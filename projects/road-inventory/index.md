## The problem

Every serious road safety decision starts with the same question: *what is out there?* How long is the network, what condition is it in, where are the intersections, the pedestrian crossings, the schools beside fast roads, the stretches with no lighting or no shoulder? Which corridors carry the most traffic, and where do people get hurt?

In Nigeria that data does not exist in any comprehensive, consistent, usable form. Agencies plan and budget without it, researchers cannot model risk without it, and it is impossible to say whether a naira spent on one road would have saved more lives on another. Nigeria carries one of the highest road-death burdens in the world and produces some of the least data about its own roads. That gap is the reason SAFER Lab exists, and closing it is where we start.

## What we are building

A **road inventory** is a structured, georeferenced record of the road network and everything on it. Ours will capture, for every segment:

{% capture col1 %}
### The road itself
Alignment and length, number of lanes, carriageway and shoulder width, surface type and condition, drainage, terrain and grade.
{% endcapture %}
{% capture col2 %}
### Safety features
Intersections and their control, pedestrian crossings and footpaths, lighting, signage and markings, speed limits and traffic calming, roadside hazards.
{% endcapture %}
{% capture col3 %}
### Use and context
Traffic volumes and mix (including motorcycles and pedestrians), land use and points of interest such as schools, markets and clinics, and crash locations where records exist.
{% endcapture %}

## How we will do it

**Local first.** We will begin with a pilot in a single local government area, working with the local authority to inventory its entire network. A complete, high-quality local dataset is worth more than a thin national one: it lets us test collection methods, prove what the data makes possible, and build the case for scaling.

**Practical collection.** Field teams with smartphone-based tools, dashcam and street-level imagery, satellite imagery and machine learning for extracting road features at scale, and existing agency records wherever they can be found and validated. We will use and contribute to open standards so the data is compatible with international road assessment practice.

**Open by default.** The inventory will be published as open data with clear documentation, so that agencies, researchers, journalists and communities can all use it. We will pair the data with simple tools: maps and dashboards that answer the questions people actually ask.

**Scale deliberately.** From one local government area to a state, then to the national network, with each step funded and staffed on the evidence from the last.

## What it makes possible

- **Better questions, answered.** Where are the highest-risk corridors for pedestrians? Which schools sit on roads with no crossing? How much of the network has no lighting?
- **Allocation planning.** Prioritising maintenance, safety countermeasures and new investment by expected lives saved rather than by guesswork.
- **Research that was not possible before.** Crash risk modelling, climate exposure mapping and readiness assessments for new mobility all depend on knowing the network.
- **Accountability.** A shared, public baseline against which progress can be measured.
