Research Themes

Road danger, a changing climate and the way cities are being rebuilt around data are not three separate problems. The same street either protects a child walking to school, holds up in a storm and serves the people who live on it, or it fails at all three. Our research is organized around these three connected challenges.

Road Safety

Crash severity and imbalanced data Making rare, severe crashes predictable

Severe and fatal crashes are rare, so models trained on raw data learn to predict “no injury”. We design synthetic data generation techniques tailored to categorical crash predictors (SMOTE-N and ADASYN-N extensions) and benchmark statistical, machine learning and deep learning severity models to establish what works under differing conditions.

Vulnerable road users People who walk, cycle and ride

Most people killed on the world’s roads were never behind the wheel. We study pedestrian and cyclist safety, safe-system road design and speed management, and the perception of risk among non-motorists, including how it shifts after high-profile crashes.

Climate Resilience

Weather-related crash risk mapping Where and when weather makes roads dangerous

A statewide workflow that aggregates crashes into space-time cubes, scores each cell with an equivalent-property-damage-only risk measure, and uses interpretable machine learning to identify, predict and explain high-risk locations across seasons.

climate-resilience machine-learning
Crash risk forecasting Forecast-conditioned deep learning

Crash history alone cannot predict weather-driven risk, but conditioning on the weather forecast restores it. A ConvLSTM-based framework for heterogeneous spatiotemporal data, with the largest gains concentrated in storm weeks.

climate-resilience machine-learning
Mountain and rural corridors Terrain, weather and adaptive infrastructure

Modeling the effect of mountainous terrain on weather-related crash rates with Tobit, latent-class Tobit and two-part models, and mapping high-risk corridors to recommend adaptive infrastructure countermeasures.

Smart Cities

Public perception of automated vehicles Trust, safety and adoption

How public safety perception shapes the adoption of automated vehicles across different communities, from non-motorists’ opinion change after a fatal AV crash to the social construction of autonomous taxis, ride-sharing and shuttles.

smart-cities automated-vehicles
Rural readiness for automated shuttles Infrastructure, vulnerability, trust and funding

Whether remote and underserved communities are equipped for autonomous shuttles, and what connected and automated vehicle data public agencies need. Includes a data readiness framework developed for the North Carolina Department of Transportation.
