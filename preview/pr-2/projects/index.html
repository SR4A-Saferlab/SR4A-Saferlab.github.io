Programs

Our programs share one design: a real community, a measurable outcome, and a plan for what happens after the grant ends. Each area below lists the kinds of projects we run and are seeking partners and funding for. We start where the injury burden is highest — children, young people and vulnerable road users in fast-growing cities — and build the data, technology and local capacity to keep the gains.

Active projects

Nigeria Road Inventory Initiative Our first project — from one local government area to a national dataset

Nigeria has no comprehensive, consistent inventory of its roads, so agencies plan without knowing what is out there and researchers cannot model risk. We are building one, starting with a pilot local government area and scaling deliberately toward national coverage, published as open data.

Injury Prevention for Young & Vulnerable People

Safe school zones Protecting children on the way to and from school

Road crashes are the leading cause of death for children over five. We work with schools, parents and local governments to audit school routes, lower speeds, add crossings and footpaths, and measure the change in speeds, near-misses and injuries — a proven, low-cost model that funders back and communities can maintain.

children speed-management
Motorcycle and rider safety Young riders, passengers and the informal transport workforce

Commercial motorcycles move millions of people across African cities and account for a growing share of serious injuries, most of them young men. We partner with riders’ unions on helmet access and quality, rider training, and safer pick-up points, and evaluate what changes behavior and reduces head injuries.

Pedestrians, traders and older adults Safety for the people who never get behind the wheel

Street traders, people crossing arterial roads and older adults face the highest risk and are rarely counted. We map where they are hurt, test safe-system fixes such as speed humps, refuge islands, lighting and market-edge design, and build the local crash and injury data that makes the problem visible.

Post-crash care and emergency response Surviving the first hour

Many road deaths happen after the crash, not at it. We study emergency response times, first-responder training and trauma referral pathways with health partners, and pilot low-cost dispatch and bystander-care programs so that a survivable injury stays survivable.

Smart & Livable Cities

Low-cost sensing and open data Seeing the city so it can be fixed

Most cities in the Global South make transport decisions with almost no data. We deploy affordable sensors, camera-based counting and mobile data collection to measure traffic, speeds, pedestrian flows and air quality, and publish the results as open data that residents, planners and journalists can use.

smart-cities open-data
Safer streets through technology Lighting, crossings and signals that serve people

Street lighting, pedestrian signals and smarter intersections are among the most cost-effective safety investments a city can make. We pilot deployments with local governments, evaluate their effect on injuries and how people use the street at night, and produce the evidence cities need to scale them.

smart-cities infrastructure
Digital tools for local planners Putting decision support in the hands of local government

Local governments rarely have the analysts or software to prioritize where to act. We build simple GIS dashboards and risk-mapping tools on top of open data, train local staff to use them, and hand them over — so technology deployment ends with a capable agency, not a vendor contract.

Resilient Communities

Flood-ready streets Keeping communities connected when the water rises

Seasonal flooding closes roads, cuts children off from school and delays emergency care in the same neighborhoods every year. We map which routes and crossings fail first, work with residents on detours and early warnings, and identify the drainage and road fixes that restore access fastest.

flooding resilience
Heat, weather and daily mobility Adapting how people move to the climate they have

Extreme heat and storms change when and how people travel and who gets hurt. We study how weather shifts risk for people walking, riding and waiting for transport, and design shade, shelter, timing and warning measures with communities that keep everyday journeys safe.

climate public-health
Community crash and hazard reporting Data from the people who live with the risk

Official crash records miss most injuries and almost all hazards. We build simple ways for residents, riders and health workers to report crashes, flooding and dangerous locations, and turn those reports into maps that local governments and funders act on.
